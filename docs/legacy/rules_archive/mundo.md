### Naming

**Passagem Cinzenta** — nome antigo e cerimonial do grande desfiladeiro que corta as Montanhas do Sul, também conhecido como **Passagem do Leste**. O nome "Cinzenta" vem do granito que domina a paisagem — cinza-escuro, quase azul quando a luz bate de lado. Nos mapas antigos da Casa Valenfort, o desfiladeiro aparece como "Passagem Cinzenta". Comerciantes e viajantes modernos passaram a chamá-la de "Passagem do Leste" — nome funcional, sem poesia. A **Companhia da Passagem Cinzenta**, fundada por Rodrigo após a queda de Valenfort, preserva o nome antigo como memória da casa.

## 100.4 Bridge Rule

Each bridge is:
- A strategic target
- Fortified (usually a tower or small keep)
- Political (tolls, control, symbolism)
- Tracked like a minor holding

**When a bridge falls:**
- Trade reroutes (+2 days travel)
- Armies slow (must find ford or ferry)
- Cities downstream lose income
- Lords upstream panic

## APPENDIX E: REGIONAL CUISINE

**Source:** Age of Shattered Oaths Rulebook V.6.7.3, Appendix E (pp. 705-708)

### E.1 Core Principle
A lord's table is a display of his land's wealth. What is served, how it is served, who sits where — all of it speaks. Empty plates speak loudest of all.

### E.2 Feast Tiers

| Tier | Cost | Food | Effect |
|:----:|:----:|:----:|--------|
| 1 — Subsistence | 10 SD | 2 FSU | Simple fare, guests notice. -1 Opinion |
| 2 — Decent | 25 SD | 4 FSU | Adequate, no shame. No modifier |
| 3 — Comfortable | 50 SD | 8 FSU | Good bread, meat, ale. +1 Opinion |
| 4 — Generous | 100 SD | 15 FSU | Multiple courses, imported goods. +2 Opinion, +1 Reputation |
| 5 — Opulent | 200+ SD | 25+ FSU | Exotic dishes, rare delicacies. +3 Opinion, +2 Reputation |

### E.3 Central Plains Cuisine
Wheat and barley breads, roasted meats (beef, pork, mutton), hearty stews with root vegetables, ale and small beer. Cheese from sheep and cattle. Apples, pears in season.

- **Lord's Table:** Multiple meats, white bread, imported wine, spiced dishes — shows agricultural wealth
- **Commoner's Table:** Brown bread, pottage (grain and vegetable stew), small beer, cheese. Meat on feast days
- **Festival Foods:** Roasted whole ox, honeyed apples, spiced ale
- **Regional Specialties:** Iron Hills lamb (mineral-rich grass), River Caedor trout (fresh, grilled with herbs), Plains cheese (aged, hard, travels well)

### E.4 Western Rivers Cuisine
Freshwater fish (salmon, trout, pike, eels), river fowl (ducks, geese), eel pies, fish stews. Imported spices from downriver trade. Salt from river estuaries. Ale, some wine.

- **Lord's Table:** Exotic fish dishes, spices from distant lands, fine wine — shows trade connections
- **Commoner's Table:** Fish stew, eel pie, dark bread, small beer. Fresh fish daily
- **Festival Foods:** The Great Fish Fry, riverboat feasts
- **Regional Specialties:** Delta eel pie (spiced, pastry-wrapped), salt-cured salmon, river mussels steamed with herbs

### E.5 Eastern Forests (Tribal) Cuisine
Game (venison, boar, rabbit), berries, nuts, maple syrup, wild herbs, mushrooms. No grains (traded for). Meat smoked or dried. Little salt — herbs for flavor.

- **Chief's Table:** Generous portions of game, maple-sweetened dishes, nuts and berries — shows hunting skill
- **Commoner's Table:** Smoked meat, dried berries, nuts, herbal teas
- **Festival Foods:** Whole roasted boar, berry feasts, maple candy for children
- **Regional Specialties:** Maple-glazed venison, dried berry cakes (travel food), mushroom and herb soup

### E.6 Eastern Forests (Settled) Cuisine
Venison, pork (domesticated pigs in forest clearings), forest mushrooms, dark beer, honey, apples. Some grains (imported or grown in clearings). Preserved meats for winter.

- **Lord's Table:** Roasted boar, venison pies, mushroom dishes, dark ale — shows mastery of both hunting and farming
- **Commoner's Table:** Pork stew, dark bread, mushrooms, small beer. Apples in season
- **Festival Foods:** Harvest feast with roasted meats, apple tarts, honey mead
- **Regional Specialties:** Forest mushroom pie, honey-roasted pork, apple brandy (distilled, strong)

### E.7 Southern Mountains Cuisine
Hardy grains (oats, barley), goat cheese and meat, stone-baked bread, mountain herbs, game, some pork. Little imported — self-sufficient. Strong ale, some whiskey.

- **Lord's Table:** Multiple cheeses, fresh bread, roasted goat or mutton, whiskey — shows self-sufficiency and herd wealth
- **Commoner's Table:** Oat porridge, goat cheese, barley bread, ale. Meat when animal slaughtered
- **Festival Foods:** Whole roasted goat, cheese wheel rolling (game), whiskey tasting
- **Regional Specialties:** Mountain goat cheese (aged in caves), stone-baked barley bread (dense, filling), heather ale

### E.8 Northern Snowlands Cuisine
Salted and preserved fish, salted meats (reindeer, seal), hardy roots (turnips, carrots), minimal spice, strong ale. Fresh food only in summer. Blood sausages, dried fish.

- **Lord's Table:** Fresh meat (rare, impressive), well-preserved stores, ale that flows — shows survival skill
- **Commoner's Table:** Dried fish, blood sausage, root stew, ale. Fresh food only in summer
- **Festival Foods:** Midwinter feast with preserved delicacies, First Catch celebration when ice breaks
- **Regional Specialties:** Dried reindeer meat (chewy, lasts years), blood sausage with herbs, cloudberry preserve (rare summer treat)

### E.9 Nomad Steppe (Traditional) Cuisine
Horse meat (honored), fermented mare's milk (airag), blood sausage, dried meat, cheese, flatbreads traded from settled folk. Little vegetable matter — herds provide.

- **Khan's Table:** Roasted horse, airag in plenty, dried meat for days — shows herd wealth and hospitality
- **Commoner's Table:** Dried meat, cheese, airag. Meat fresh when animal slaughtered
- **Festival Foods:** The Great Roast (whole horse), airag drinking contests, dried meat sharing
- **Regional Specialties:** Roasted horse (honored dish), airag (fermented mare's milk), dried meat strips (travel food)

### E.10 Nomad Steppe (Settled) Cuisine
Blended traditions: horse meat and rice, grilled meats with spices, pilafs, bread, pastries influenced by settled neighbors. Airag still served, now in fine cups.

- **Khan-King's Table:** Best of both worlds — steppe abundance and settled refinement
- **Commoner's Table:** Rice pilaf with meat, flatbreads, tea. Airag on special occasions
- **Festival Foods:** Fusion feasts with both steppe and settled dishes
- **Regional Specialties:** Spiced horse meat with rice (steppe-settled fusion), fried bread with honey, tea with milk and salt
