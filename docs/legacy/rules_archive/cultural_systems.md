## PART 17: WAR HORNS (Pages 88-91)

### 17.1 Core Principle
> "When a horn calls, blades rise. Oaths are tested. In this world, the bloodground around them is covered in broken, discarded oaths. The horn remembers what men forget."

A lord's personal horn is passed down through generations. A silent horn means a lord has fallen — or his word has broken.

### 17.2 Horn Types

| Horn Type | Sound | Used By | Materials |
|---|---|---|---|
| Battle Horn | Deep, urgent | Lords, commanders | Bronze, iron, aurochs horn |
| War Horn | Long, rising | Armies | Bronze, wood, ox horn |
| Hunting Horn | Quick, bright | Hunters | Cow horn |

### 17.3 Horn Crafting

| Horn Type | Materials | Cost (SD) | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hunting Horn | Cow horn, leather wrap | 10 | 3 days |
| Battle Horn | Bronze, iron bands | 40 | 2 weeks |
| War Horn | Bronze, wood, leather | 60 | 1 month |
| Clan Horn | Elk horn, carved | 80 | 1 month |
| Oath-Horn | Silver, ancient design | 200 | 3 months |
| Mourning Horn | Raw horn, unadorned | 5 | 1 day |

### 17.4 Horn Calls and Effects

| Call | Meaning | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| One long blast | Assembly | All units within earshot gain +1 Initiative next round |
| Two short blasts | Attack | +1 Morale on next Charge |
| Three short blasts | Retreat | -1 to enemy pursuit rolls |
| Rising then falling | Lord's presence | +1 Loyalty to all allied commanders present |
| Single low note | Mourning | -1 Morale, +1 to vengeance rolls next battle |
| Repeated pattern | Recognition | Enemy must check morale if they recognize the horn |

### 17.5 The Horn of Oaths

- **Oath-Breaker's Horn:** If a lord breaks a sworn oath, his horn cannot be used until he atones. Attempts to sound it produce only a dull, dead note. All who hear know what it means.
- **Horn Breaking:** Destroying an enemy's horn in battle. Horn-breaker gains **+1 Reputation** (Cruel or Mighty). Horn's owner loses **-1 Reputation** and **-1 Loyalty** from all who witnessed it.
- **Horn as Trophy:** Capturing an enemy lord's horn grants **+1 Reputation**. Returning it (ransom or gesture) grants **+2 Opinion** with that lord's house.

## PART 18: SMUDGING & SHARING CIRCLE (Pages 91-95)

### 18.1 Core Principle
> "The smoke carries what needs to leave — anger, grief, fear, the weight of unkindness. It does not destroy these things. It lifts them, transforms them, returns them to the air where they can become something else."

### 18.2 Smudging Materials

| Material | Source | Cost (SD) | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sage (bundle) | Eastern Forests, Plains | 3 | Cleansing, purification |
| Cedar (bundle) | Eastern Forests | 4 | Protection, prayers |
| Sweetgrass | Plains, Meadows | 5 | Kindness, healing |
| Tobacco (loose) | Trade, Southern | 2 | Offering, gratitude |

### 18.3 The Sharing Circle
Circle has no beginning and no end. All are equal within it. The circle moves clockwise, following the sun's path.

### 18.4 The Talking Stick
An object is placed in the center — a carved stick, a feather, a stone. Passed clockwise. Whoever holds it speaks. No one interrupts. No one prepares their response while another talks.

### 18.5 The Seven Smudging Gestures

| Gesture | Words | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Eyes | "To see good things" | To witness the world with kindness, to see beauty even in hardship |
| Ears | "To hear good things" | To listen deeply, to hear the truth beneath words |
| Heart | "To love openly" | To keep the heart soft, to love even when it hurts |
| Mouth/Breath | "To speak good things" | To let words carry goodness, to speak truth with care |
| Head | "To have good thoughts" | To think clearly, to hold good intentions |
| Legs/Feet | "To walk a good path" | To move through the world with purpose, to leave good footprints |
| Whole Body | "To carry goodness" | To carry goodness with you wherever you go |

### 18.6 When They Gather

| Occasion | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Grief | To let the smoke carry what is too heavy to hold alone |
| Council | To speak clearly and listen deeply before decisions |
| Celebration | To give thanks, to witness joy together |
| Conflict | To cool anger, to remember they are still family |
| Weekly Gathering | To catch up, to be seen, to know they are not alone |
| Before Ceremony | To prepare the spirit for what comes |

### 18.8 Mechanical Effects

| Situation | Effect |
|---|---|
| Before a ceremony | +1 to ceremony effectiveness (the mind is clear) |
| Before a difficult conversation | +1 to diplomacy (intention is set) |

### 18.9 Individual Smudging
A person may smudge alone, at dawn or dusk. Same effect as group ceremony — it is simply private.

## PART 19: TRIBAL FAMILY SYSTEMS (Pages 95-99 + Patch 7.1)

### 19.2 Family Reputation (Per Patch 7.1)

| Roll 1d6 | Reputation | Effect | Wealth | Food | Special |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Selfish | -1 to tribe loyalty, hoards resources | 1.5x wealth | Poor (0.5x FSU) | Hated by neighbors |
| 2 | Struggling | Trying but failing, may need help | 0.5x wealth | Barely surviving | Refugees may be turned away |
| 3 | Neutral | No strong reputation | 1.0x wealth | Adequate | — |
| 4 | Generous | +1 to trade with other families | 0.8x wealth (gave away) | Plentiful (1.5x FSU) | Loved by neighbors |
| 5 | Honored | Known for kindness, +1 to diplomacy | 1.2x wealth | Plentiful | +1 to diplomacy |
| 6 | Legendary | Stories told about them, +2 to all family interactions | 2.0x wealth | Abundant | May adopt worthy strangers |

### 19.3 Family Loyalty (0-6)

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0-1 | Resentful, may rebel |
| 2-3 | Neutral, follow but don't volunteer |
| 4-5 | Loyal, contribute willingly |
| 6 | Devoted, would die for the chief |

### 19.4 Family Council

**Resolution:** Roll **2d6 + Chief's Reputation with Families**

| Roll | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 2-4 | Families openly oppose. Loyalty -2, possible rebellion |
| 5-7 | Families grumble but comply. Loyalty -1 |
| 8-10 | Families support. Loyalty +1 |
| 11-12 | Families embrace decision. Loyalty +2, offer additional support |

### 19.5 Hospitality and The Stranger

| Family Reputation | Response |
|---|---|
| Selfish (1) | Turn refugees away (-1 tribe loyalty, refugees remember) |
| Struggling (2) | Want to help but can't — send refugees to next camp |
| Neutral (3) | Debate. Roll family council. |
| Generous (4) | Accept refugees, share food (-1 FSU, +1 tribe reputation) |
| Honored (5) | Welcome refugees, give generously (-2 FSU, +2 reputation) |
| Legendary (6) | Adopt refugees as family (-3 FSU, +3 reputation, possible new family member) |

## PART 20: THE EIGHT TRADITIONS (Pages 99-100)

> "Some men are crowned not because they lift the crown to their head, but because other men place it there and bend."

The old king wore eight crowns — one for each tradition. When he died, they returned to their eight peoples. Now they wait for someone who can gather them again.

The Eight Crowns:
1. **Crown of Blood** — Central Plains (War King)
2. **Crown of Contracts** — Western Rivers (Charter King)
3. **Crown of Roots** — Eastern Forests Tribal (Sachem)
4. **Crown of the Green Drake** — Eastern Forests Settled (Lord of the High Mound)
5. **Crown of Stone** — Southern Mountains (Blood King)
6. **Crown of the North Wind** — Northern Snowlands (Lord of the High Seat)
7. **The Felt Crown** — Nomad Steppe Traditional (Khan)
8. **The Settled Crown** — Nomad Steppe Transformed (Transformed Khan)

## PART 21: CROWN OF BLOOD — CENTRAL PLAINS (Pages 100-102 + Patch 7.4)

**Title:** War King
**Throne:** The Iron Seat

### 21.1 Kingship Triggers
A claimant may be recognized as War King if they:
- Win **3+ major battles** in the Central Plains
- Defeat the **previous War King** in battle
- Command the loyalty of **5+ great houses** of the Plains

### 21.3 War King Legitimacy Track

| Battles Won | Legitimacy Score | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | Aspirant |
| 2 | 2 | Contender |
| 3 | 3 | Recognized in home region |
| 4 | 4 | Known abroad |
| 5+ | 5 | Undeniable |

### 21.4 Challenge Mechanics
Any lord with an **equal or greater** War King score may challenge the sitting king. Challenge must be **public**. Refusal reduces Opinion by **-2 with all houses**. If accepted, the two armies meet — or the two kings meet in **single combat** if both agree.

### 21.5 No Inheritance
Children do **not** inherit. They must win it themselves. A son of a king starts with **+1 Reputation** (name is known) but no automatic claim.

### 21.6 The Iron Seat's Domain (Per Patch 7.4)
Direct domain: only **200 miles across**. Beyond lie march lords, border counts, wild lands.
- **Travel Time Across Direct Domain:** 2-3 weeks

## PART 22: CROWN OF CONTRACTS — WESTERN RIVERS (Pages 102-104 + Patch 7.4)

**Title:** Charter King
**Throne:** The Toll Throne

### 22.1 Kingship Triggers
A claimant may be recognized as Charter King if they:
- Secure trade agreements with **75% of major river holdings**
- Are **elected by the River Council**
- Receive the **signed charter** of participating lords

### 22.3 River Council Mechanics
Council meets when a king dies or a claimant demands recognition. Uses the Grand Ball framework for attendance and influence. Each attending lord may vote by signed charter.

**Council Attendance Roll:** 2d6 + Relations modifiers

| Roll | Outcome |
|---|---|
| 2-4 | Few attend (legitimacy questioned) |
| 5-8 | Moderate attendance |
| 9-11 | Nearly all attend (strong mandate) |
| 12 | Every lord attends (unprecedented) |

### 22.4 Breach of Charter
If a king violates the charter (raising tolls without consent, seizing merchant goods, breaking trade agreements), any signatory lord may withdraw support. If **more than half** withdraw, the king may be deposed.

### 22.5 Succession by Election
Any lord may be considered, regardless of blood. Merchant princes with sufficient wealth may also stand.

### 22.6 The Toll Throne's Rivers (Per Patch 7.4)
3 major river systems, each **300-500 miles** long.
- **Downstream:** 3-4 weeks
- **Upstream (poling/towing):** 6-8 weeks

## PART 23: CROWN OF ROOTS — EASTERN FORESTS (TRIBAL) (Pages 104-106 + Patch 7.4)

**Title:** Sachem
**Throne:** The Living Stool (a living stump, still growing, still rooted)

### 23.1 Kingship Triggers
A claimant may be recognized as Sachem if they:
- Are chosen by the **Forest Council of elders**
- Show signs of **acceptance from the land**
- Are recognized by the people as one who carries their will

### 23.3 Forest Council Mechanics
Elders from each family. They do **not vote** — they speak until consensus emerges.

| Council Size | Time to Consensus |
|---|---|
| Small tribe (3-4 families) | 1-3 days |
| Medium tribe (5-7 families) | 3-7 days |
| Large tribe (8+ families) | 7-14 days |

### 23.4 Walking the Land Ceremony
A claimant must walk the sacred places of the forest **alone**, with only their knowledge of the land to guide them. If they complete the walk, they return. If they do not, the forest has taken them.

### 23.5 Signs of Acceptance
- Game appears where there was none
- Water flows from springs long dry
- Trees bloom out of season
- Wind carries whispers of approval

### 23.6 Land's Rejection
If a Sachem acts against the forest (cutting sacred groves, overhunting, ignoring elders): signs reverse, game grows scarce, springs dry, loyalty drops.

### 23.7 The Living Stool's Forest (Per Patch 7.4)
Eastern Forests: **600 miles north-south**.
- **Messenger travel (north to south):** 6-8 weeks in summer
- **Winter:** Impossible (deep snow, frozen rivers)

## PART 24: CROWN OF THE GREEN DRAKE — EASTERN FORESTS (SETTLED) (Pages 106-109)

**Title:** Lord of the High Mound
**Throne:** Hill Forts

### 24.1 The Green Drake Myth
A man called the Green Drake fought a dragon that threatened to swallow the world. He killed it but lost his leg. The spirits raised the Eastern Forest around him. His last breath became the eastern wind.

### 24.2 Kingship Triggers
A claimant may be recognized as Lord of the High Mound if they:
- Carry the **blood of the Green Drake** (can be traced, however distantly)
- Prove skill with the **bow in the Great Hunt**
- Are **whispered of by the wind** (high reputation among the folk)

### 24.3 The Three Signs

| Sign | Evidence | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Blood | Descent from the Green Drake | Can be traced, even distantly |
| Bow | Proven hunting skill | Must have taken a great beast in fair chase |
| Whisper | The wind speaks of you | Reputation ≥ 6, Opinion ≥ +2 across multiple forest lords |

### 24.4 Hill Fort Upgrades

| Upgrade | Cost (SD) | Time | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| The High Mound | 200 SD | 1 year | +1 to all ceremonial rolls |
| The Bowyer's Grove | 150 SD | 6 months | +1 to hunting, can train elite archers |
| The Hunting Lodge | 100 SD | 3 months | +1 to hunting, young lords train faster |

### 24.5 The Great Hunt
When a king dies, claimants hunt alone for **three days**, with only bow and skill.

| Hunt Result | Outcome |
|---|---|
| No kill | Not worthy — cannot be king |
| Small game (rabbit, fowl) | Minimal respect, unlikely to win |
| Medium game (deer, boar) | Respectable, possible contender |
| Great beast (stag, bear, wolf) | Strong claim |

### 24.6 The Whisper Test
Before confirmation, the claimant must spend **a night alone** at the Green Drake's Birch. If he hears the whispers of his ancestors or the Green Drake himself, he may return and be crowned.

### 24.7 Relations with Tribal Folk

| Relation | Opinion Modifier |
|---|---|
| Trade partners | +2 base |
| Intermarried families | +3 base |
| Recent conflict | -2 for 1 generation |
| Shared ceremony attendance | +1 for 1 year |

## PART 25: CROWN OF STONE — SOUTHERN MOUNTAINS (Pages 109-111 + Patch 7.4)

**Title:** Blood King
**Throne:** The Stone Seat (carved from the mountain itself)

### 25.1 Kingship Triggers
Must be recognized through **direct bloodline inheritance**. The crown passes from father to son, mother to daughter.

### 25.3 Ancestor Recognition
New king must visit the ancestral tombs carved into the mountain's heart. They walk among the bones. If accepted: a presence, a peace. If rejected: the mountain feels empty, cold, unwelcoming.

### 25.4 Blood Purity

| Relation | Purity Score |
|---|---|
| Direct child | 10 |
| Grandchild | 8 |
| Sibling | 7 |
| Niece/nephew | 5 |
| Distant relation | 3 |

**Purity ≥ 5:** "of the blood" — can inherit.
**Purity < 5:** outsider — cannot inherit, must conquer.

### 25.5 Usurper Mechanics
Someone not of the blood can only rule by conquest. They suffer:
- **-2 Opinion** from all mountain houses
- Constant rebellion risk
- **No inheritance** — their children are not considered blood

### 25.6 The Stone Seat's Mountains (Per Patch 7.4)
Southern Mountains: a labyrinth of ranges and valleys.
- **Using known passes:** 6-8 weeks
- **Exploring new routes:** 3-4 months, with high risk

## PART 26: CROWN OF THE NORTH WIND — NORTHERN SNOWLANDS (Patch 34.0/35.0)

**Title:** Lord of the High Seat
**Throne:** The High Seat (highest chair in the great hall)

### 26.1 Cultural Identity
- **Highland clan roots:** fierce family loyalty, blood feuds
- **Norman conqueror pragmatism:** rule by the sword, administer and build
- Welcoming but judging — shares fire and food, but watches all night

### 26.2 Kingship Triggers
Must satisfy **at least two** of the Threefold Right:

| Right | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Blood | Claim to the line (even distant) |
| Steel | 3+ major victories OR conquest of key holding |
| Word | No proven oathbreaking; at least one major oath kept at personal cost |

### 26.4 The Gathering

| Step | Description |
|---|---|
| The Speaking | Each lord speaks what they know of the claimants. Truth is expected. |
| The Standing | Claimants stand and give their word — what they will protect, what they will sacrifice. |
| The Choosing | Lords step forward and stand behind the one they choose. |
| The Acclamation | The claimant with the most lords behind them is king. |

### 26.5 Northern Speech

| A Northerner Says... | A Southerner Hears... | What It Actually Means |
|---|---|---|
| "That's a bold plan." | "He approves." | "That plan might kill us all, but I'm willing to watch." |
| "You talk well." | "He respects my eloquence." | "You talk too much. Show me what you can do." |
| "Sit by the fire." | "He welcomes me." | "I'm watching you. The fire makes it easier." |
| "We'll see." | "He's considering it." | "No. But I have reasons for not saying no directly." |
| (Silence) | "He has nothing to say." | "I have much to say. None of it is for your ears." |

### 26.7 The Ward's Silence

| Situation | Expected Response | Mechanical Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Insult at court | Smile, nod, say nothing | +1 to Patience (tracked internally) |
| Child treated poorly | Observe, document, wait | -1 Opinion (hidden), +2 to future vengeance |
| Child endangered | Immediate action, silence broken | Risk of feud, but child comes first |
| Wardship ends | Lord collects child, rides north | All stored insults available for response |

### 26.8 Survival is the Only Law
A king who cannot keep his people alive through winter has no right to rule. When a king fails, the lords simply stop coming when he calls.

### 26.9 The High Seat's Snowlands (Per Patch 7.4)
Most sparsely populated region. Journey from Harald's Hold (west) to eastern fishing villages:
- **Summer:** 5-6 weeks
- **Winter:** 3-4 months (if possible at all)

## PART 27: THE FELT CROWN — NOMAD STEPPE (TRADITIONAL) (Pages 115-117)

**Title:** Khan
**Throne:** A horse (mobile throne — the Orda moves with seasons and herds)
**Crown:** A felt blanket

### 27.1 Kingship Triggers
A claimant may be recognized as Khan if they:
- Unite **75%+ of steppe clans** as vassals
- Are confirmed by **Kurultai**
- Are **lifted on the felt** by clan chiefs

### 27.2 The Felt Crown
> "You are light enough to carry. Now carry us."
A Khan who forgets this becomes heavy. Heavy Khans fall.

### 27.3 Kurultai Confirmation
Gathering of all clan chiefs at a sacred ground. Claimant must have support of **at least two-thirds** of chiefs.

| Kurultai Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Unanimous support | Khan's authority unchallenged, **+2 Loyalty** all clans |
| Two-thirds support | Khan confirmed, but opposition clans resentful (**-1 Loyalty**) |
| Bare majority | Khan confirmed but weak, constant challenges |

### 27.5 Succession by Kurultai
When a Khan dies, the Kurultai gathers again. Blood matters, but so does ability. The chiefs choose the one most likely to keep the horde united.

## PART 28: THE SETTLED CROWN — NOMAD STEPPE (TRANSFORMED) (Pages 117-120)

### 28.1 Transformation Trigger
A Khan may begin transformation to settled rule if they:
- Control at least **one city or castle for 5+ years**
- Establish a **permanent capital**
- Begin using **settled administration**

### 28.2 Cultural Conversion Stages

| Stage | Time | Effect | Resistance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conquest | Year 1 | Khan rules settled land but lives in Orda | High (clans suspicious) |
| Tribute | Year 2-3 | Khan collects taxes, uses scribes | Moderate |
| Integration | Year 4-5 | Khan's children educated both ways | Low |
| Transformation | Year 6+ | Khan holds both titles, rules from palace | Minimal |

### 28.3 Conversion Tools

| Tool | Mechanics | Cultural Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Economic Ties | Trade agreements, tribute | Settled goods become desirable |
| Political Marriage | Marriage alliances | Families blend, loyalties blur |
| Religious Syncretism | Blend faiths | Spiritual unity, less resistance |

### 28.4 The Cost of Transformation

| Loss | Effect |
|---|---|
| Traditionalist clans break away | -X loyalty, possible rebellion |
| Steppe identity dilutes | Future generations may not remember the old ways |
| Settled lords still suspicious | Never fully trusted by either world |
| The Felt Crown feels heavier | Khan now carries two peoples, not one |

### 28.5 What They Become

| Region | Title for Transformed Khan | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Central Plains | "The Horse Lord" | Respectful, wary — he came on a horse, now he stays |
| Western Rivers | "The Tribute Khan" | Mercantile — he collects taxes now, like us |
| Eastern Forests | "The Two-Path One" | Curious — he walks both ways |
| Southern Mountains | "The Usurper" | Dismissive — no blood, no crown |
| Northern Snowlands | "The Survivor" | Practical — he adapted. We respect that. |
| Steppe (traditionalists) | "The Lost One" | Tragic — he forgot the grass |

## PART 29: THE RUBICON CROWN — ARMY PROCLAMATION (Pages 120-123)

### 29.2 The Rubicon Threshold

| Factor | Requirement | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Loyalty | Average commander loyalty ≥ 4 | +1 per point above 3 |
| Victories | 5+ major battles won | +1 per 2 victories |
| Morale | Army morale "Exalted" for 3+ weeks | +1 per week |
| Banner Tier | Commander's personal banner Tier 4+ | +2 |
| Reputation | Commander's reputation ≥ 7 | +2 |
| War Will | Army War Will ≥ 8 | +1 |
| Shared Sacrifice | Army has endured a near-disaster together | +2 (once) |
| The Whisper | Leader has planted the thought | +1 to +3 |

**Rubicon Score = Sum of all factors.**
**Threshold: 12+**

### 29.3 The Whisper — Planting the Thought

| Technique | Effect |
|---|---|
| Victory Speeches over multiple battles | +1 |
| Leading from the front, sharing hardship | +1 |
| Talking of "what comes after" | +1 |
| "When I am king..." in private conversations | +1 |

### 29.4 The Crossing — Moment of Proclamation

When Rubicon Score reaches **12**, the moment can happen.

**If the leader does nothing:** The army acts. Roll **2d6**.

| Roll | The Proclamation |
|---|---|
| 2-4 | Hesitant — "Some of us have been talking..." |
| 5-8 | Firm — "The men have decided. You are our king." |
| 9-11 | Overwhelming — Entire army shouts as one |
| 12 | Legendary — They crown you on the spot with whatever is at hand |

**If the leader refuses:** Roll **2d6 + Rubicon Score**.

| Roll | Outcome |
|---|---|
| ≤ 8 | They accept. But something breaks. **-2 Loyalty** |
| 9-11 | They argue. Loyalty split. |
| 12+ | They refuse your refusal. "You are our king whether you will it or not." |

### 29.5 Aftermath

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| All proclaiming units | Loyalty becomes **6 (fanatic)** permanently |
| Morale | **+2 Morale** for all proclaiming units |
| Recruitment | **+1d6×10** recruits per season |
| Reputation | **+3 Reputation**, possible new nickname |
| Other lords Opinion | **-2** with all non-proclaiming lords |
| Other kings | Relations drop to **-3** (rival claimants) |
| Former liege | If any, they are now enemy |

### 29.6 The Rubicon King's Legitimacy
No legitimacy by any traditional crown. Authority rests on:
- The army that proclaimed them
- Fear (other lords may bend to avoid being crushed)
- Time (if they rule long enough, people forget how it started)
- Marriage into a legitimate line
- Conquest of a capital (adds Crown of Blood)
- Recognition by an existing king

## PART 30: THE BENDING (Pages 123-125 + Patch 7.3)

### 30.1 The Bend Defined
The formal act of swearing fealty to a regional king. A lord kneels, speaks his oath, and rises a vassal.

### 30.2 Bending Triggers
A lord may choose to bend when:
- **Opinion of the king ≥ +2**
- **Fear** (army at gates, no hope of victory)
- **Mutual benefit** (trade, protection, alliance)

### 30.3 Public Ceremony
The lord kneels, places his hands between the king's hands, and speaks:
> "I, [name] of [house], do swear fealty to [king name], King of [crown]. I shall be his man, in winter and summer, in war and peace, in life and death. This I swear by my sword, my land, and the honor of my house. So hear me."

### 30.4 Reluctant Bend
If a lord bends only out of fear:
- **Mechanical Effect:** Loyalty starts at **1** instead of 3. Must be earned.

### 30.5 Broken Bend
If a vassal rebels ("broken their bend"):
- **-3 Reputation** (oathbreaker)
- Former liege gains **Claim**
- All other lords **-1 Opinion** (if you'll betray him, you'll betray me)

### 30.6 Multiple Bends
A lord cannot bend to multiple kings. If discovered, the king may:
- Demand they renounce the other
- Seize lands
- Declare war

### 30.7 The Feudal Web at Game Start (Per Patch 7.3)

```
Level 0: THE EMPTY THRONE
├── 16 Great Lords (sworn to crown, now independent in practice)
│   ├── 80 Minor Lords (sworn to Great Lords)
```

## PART 31: THE EMPTY THRONE (Pages 125-127)

### 31.1 The Old King's Crown
The last king to wear all eight crowns died **thirty years ago**. His children had predeceased him. No one could agree who should rule. The eight crowns returned to their eight peoples. The realm learned to live without a king.

### 31.2 The Hidden Heir
Some say a daughter survived — hidden in the mountains, raised in secret, a woman now with the old king's eyes. Others say she died with the rest.

> "The truth waits in the Southern Mountains, in a fortress no map shows, guarded by knights who have kept their oath for thirty years."

### 31.3 The Great Reconciliation
If all eight crowns are gathered on one head, the Empty Throne is filled. The one who wears them may call themselves **High King** — but only if:
1. They hold **all eight crowns**
2. They are recognized by **at least half** the great houses of each region
3. They can **defend the throne** against all challengers

This has not happened since the old king died. It may never happen again.
